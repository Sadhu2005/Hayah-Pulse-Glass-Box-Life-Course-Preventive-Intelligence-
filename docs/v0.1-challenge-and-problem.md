## 1. The Challenge

How might we identify children, adults and communities with unmet or elevated health risk and connect them to the right preventive intervention—Well-Child Visit or Adult comprehensive screening pathway—at the right time, through approaches that are personalized and culturally appropriate?

---

## 2. The Problem

Abu Dhabi’s strategic direction is to move from **reactive care** to **predictive prevention and early detection** across the life course, using population health intelligence to convert longitudinal health and genomic data into action.

Today, eligibility and outreach for the Well-Child Visit program, adult comprehensive screening, and other preventive pathways can still depend heavily on broad **age- and gender-based rules**. Clinically important risk, however, is distributed across:

- Family history
- Genomic information
- Chronic conditions
- Developmental and behavioral factors
- Screening and visit history
- Geography
- Social or environmental context

As a result, children, adults, and families with the highest unmet need may be:

- **Missed** entirely
- **Invited too late**
- **Directed to a pathway** that does not fit their circumstances

A useful challenge solution must translate insight into timely enrolment, recall, or follow-up through the appropriate Well-Child Visit, adult comprehensive screening, or preventive pathway—while making **uncertainty** and **authorized data use** visible.

---

## 3. Objective

Use relevant health and genomic insights to identify children, adults, and communities with unmet or elevated health risk and connect them, at the right time, to the appropriate Well-Child Visit, adult comprehensive screening, or preventive intervention.

---

## 4. Desired Outcomes

### 4.1 Transparent risk and care-gap identification

Use authorized clinical, genomic, developmental, behavioral, family-history, Well-Child Visit, adult screening, and contextual data to identify elevated or unmet risk and missed or overdue care, showing the major factors behind each flag.

### 4.2 Personalized preventive pathways

Recommend the appropriate Well-Child Visit, adult comprehensive screening, test, or preventive intervention—including timing, recall, and follow-up—within approved guidelines, while clearly distinguishing **population prioritization** from **individual clinical decision-making**.

### 4.3 Culturally appropriate engagement

Tailor language, channel, and message to children, parents, caregivers, and adults, while giving individuals and families a meaningful choice about participation.

### 4.4 Local population safeguards

Assess reach, false-positive and false-negative patterns, recommendation rates, and uptake across relevant segments of the local target population; suppress small cells and avoid misleading results when data is insufficient.

### 4.5 Outcome-based evaluation

Measure completed Well-Child Visits, adult comprehensive screenings, and preventive actions, together with timely attendance, recall and follow-up completion, model calibration, and downstream clinical value.

---

## 5. Success lens for the prototype

The prototype must demonstrably show that:

1. Risk flags are driven by more than age/sex calendars.
2. Every flag exposes auditable reasons and a confidence/uncertainty signal.
3. Families see why they were invited and can choose how to engage.
4. Aggregate views suppress small cells (`n < 10`) and surface equity metrics.
